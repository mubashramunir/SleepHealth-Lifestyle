# Sleep Health and Lifestyle: Analysis Report

An exploratory data analysis of how stress, lifestyle and health factors relate to sleep quality and sleep disorders, built in Python with Pandas, Matplotlib and Seaborn.

Completed for **BSBINS401 – Introduction to Data Visualisation Tools** (ICT40120 Certificate IV in Information Technology – Data Science and AI).

---

## Overview

Poor sleep affects concentration, health and productivity. This project analyses the [Sleep Health and Lifestyle Dataset](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/uom190346a/sleep-health-and-lifestyle-dataset) (374 working adults, 11 occupations) to help a workplace wellbeing team understand which factors are most closely linked to poor sleep, so support programs can be targeted where they will have the most effect.

### Research objectives

1. Determine how stress and lifestyle factors (sleep duration, physical activity, daily steps) relate to sleep quality.
2. Identify which health factors, particularly BMI category, are linked to sleep disorders (Insomnia and Sleep Apnea).
3. Compare sleep quality across occupations to find the groups most at risk.

---

## Key findings

| # | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---------|----------|
| 1 | **Stress is the strongest link to poor sleep.** Correlation with sleep quality is r = −0.90. 70% of high-stress people (level 7+) have a sleep disorder, compared with 28% of low-stress people. | Figures 1–2 |
| 2 | **BMI is the strongest link to sleep disorders.** 7% of people with a Normal BMI have a disorder, rising to 87% for Overweight and 100% for Obese (small group, n = 10). | Figure 3 |
| 3 | **Sleep quality differs by occupation, in line with stress.** Engineers have the best sleep (median 9) and lowest stress; Salespeople and Nurses have the lowest median (6) among the larger groups. | Figure 4 |

Sleep duration is also strongly linked to sleep quality (r = +0.88), while daily steps (r = 0.02) and physical activity (r = 0.19) show only weak links.

### Recommendations

- **Stress management** – workload reviews, flexible scheduling and wellbeing sessions, starting with the highest-stress roles.
- **Healthy weight support** – health checks, weight-management programs and Sleep Apnea screening.
- **Ongoing monitoring** – add sleep and stress questions to staff wellbeing surveys.

## Data cleaning

- Filled 219 missing `Sleep Disorder` values with `"None"` (they represent people without a disorder).
- Merged the duplicate BMI labels `"Normal Weight"` and `"Normal"`.
- Split `Blood Pressure` (e.g. `126/83`) into numeric `Systolic` and `Diastolic` columns.
- Checked duplicates (none exact) and outliers (15 Heart Rate values of 80–86 bpm, kept as realistic).
- Added helper columns: `Stress Group` (High = 7+) and `Has Disorder`.

The original CSV is never modified; the cleaned copy is saved as a separate file.

## Getting started

### Requirements

- Python 3.9+
- Jupyter Notebook or JupyterLab
- pandas, numpy, matplotlib, seaborn

```bash
pip install pandas numpy matplotlib seaborn jupyter
```

### Run the notebook

1. Clone this repository.
2. Download `Sleep Health and Lifestyle Dataset.csv` from [Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/uom190346a/sleep-health-and-lifestyle-dataset) and place it in the project folder.
3. Launch Jupyter and open the notebook:
   ```bash
   jupyter notebook "Sleep Health Report.ipynb"
   ```
4. Run all cells (**Kernel → Restart & Run All**).

## Limitations

- Results show **associations, not cause and effect**.
- The dataset is small, and some occupations have fewer than 5 people.
- The dataset is **synthetic** and intended for illustration, so findings should be confirmed with real data before informing decisions.
